# Love & Muhammad: Defining Literacies

## Readable Themed Overview

This source helps Module 2 define literacy as more than reading and writing. Love and Muhammad push literacy toward identity, history, justice, and human development. Literacy is not only a school skill; it is how students understand themselves, their communities, their histories, and the world around them.

Through the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS lens, this source matters because students are often misread when schools only value narrow academic performance. A student may be literate through storytelling, cultural knowledge, oral language, family history, digital practice, movement, art, or survival knowledge. Culturally and historically responsive literacy asks teachers to see those literacies as part of learning rather than as background noise.

The reading also connects to the course theme because it refuses deficit thinking. Students are not empty containers waiting for school to fill them. They already carry knowledge, language, memory, and meaning. Teaching should build from that fullness.

## Teaching Takeaway

Teach literacy as identity, meaning-making, and justice. Students should see themselves, their histories, and their communities as part of the curriculum.
