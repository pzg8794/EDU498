## The Central Question

Sealey-Ruiz opens with one image: what if every child who walked into a school building felt loved by their teacher, their principal, their classmates? That question is the entire argument. The system fails Black and Brown children not because they lack genius, but because their teachers lack racial literacy.

---

## Archaeology of Self

Racial literacy starts with the teacher, not the student. Sealey-Ruiz calls this the Archaeology of Self — a deep excavation of what you actually believe about race, intelligence, and whose humanity counts. She uses the image of a shovel deliberately. The digging is hard. Radical honesty is uncomfortable. But examining, excavating, and interrupting harmful beliefs is empowering, not punishing.

> "I have decided to live a life of excavation." (Sealey-Ruiz, 2022)

## The Six Components of the Racial Literacy Development Model

1. Critical love
2. Critical humility
3. Critical reflection
4. Historical literacy
5. Archaeology of Self
6. The act of interruption

## The Mrs. Kipling Story

A white toddler, caught eating cakes, blamed "a black man." Her mother laughed. That laugh was the lesson — it affirmed a racist stereotype and passed it on. The mother's racial literacy had never been developed, so she missed the moment entirely. Sealey-Ruiz uses this story to show that harmful beliefs do not stay private. They become classroom practice. They become policy.

---

## Connection to Our Theme

Sealey-Ruiz makes the same argument reflected in the freewrite and slogan — from the teacher's side. The slogan says: your identity is the key to literacy. She says: teachers must excavate their own beliefs before they can recognize that key in their students.

A teacher who has not done the internal work will see a student's neurodivergence, Blackness, multilingualism, or complex identity as a problem to manage. A racially literate teacher sees it as the starting point for learning. Belonging before building applies to the teacher first.

---

## Her Closing Commitment

"I have decided to live a life of excavation." That is the standard she sets. Not a workshop. Not a certification. A life practice of truth, self-reflection, and love — because that is what Black and Brown children deserve from every adult in their building.
