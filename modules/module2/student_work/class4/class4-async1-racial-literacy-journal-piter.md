# Class 4: Asynchronous Activity 1 Journal
## Unearthing What We've Learned: A Reflective Excavation of Racial Belief
**Name:** Piter
**Class:** EDU498 · Class 4 · Culturally-Historically Responsive Literacies
**Date:** May 28, 2026

---

## Describe the moment
One moment that still stays with me happened during my first semester in the United States. I was in a class discussion and shared my interpretation of a reading. Before anyone engaged my argument, the attention moved to my accent and language delivery. The room shifted from ideas to whether I sounded academic enough. In that moment, I felt my intelligence had to be translated before it could be heard.

---

## Excavate the moment
The belief underneath that moment was that legitimacy has a single sound: White, monolingual, and institutionally familiar. The assumption ranked language, race, and status before ideas. It decided who gets read as naturally intelligent and who must prove intelligence first. This belief comes from schooling histories that treat dominant academic English as neutral and treat bilingual speakers as people who need correction.

---

## Reflect on the impact of the moment
The immediate impact was contraction. I spoke less, edited myself more, and moved energy from learning to self-monitoring. Over time, that pattern shaped belonging. I learned to split parts of myself depending on the room. Looking back, I read that moment as institutional pattern recognition. I now understand how language gatekeeping can shrink participation even when no one names race directly.

---

## Interrupt the moment
My interruption commitment is to design classrooms where students can show full identity and still be recognized as capable. In practice, I name multiple language practices and communication modes as legitimate literacy. I assess reasoning before fluency performance. I build participation structures that protect voice diversity. I also practice Sealey-Ruiz's archaeology of self continuously. I examine how I interpret behavior, speech, and readiness so my teaching decisions support equity.

---

## Pattern statement
Recurring pattern: institutions reward identity flattening before granting academic legitimacy.
Chosen interruption target: teacher noticing and assessment language.
