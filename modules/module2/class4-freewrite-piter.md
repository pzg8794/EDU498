---

## How was literacy and language cultivated historically with your family/ancestors?

Literacy in my family was cultivated through love and intentionality. My mother noticed early on that my brain worked differently from other children’s, not as a deficit but as something that required a different kind of attention. My mother took it upon herself to teach us not just content, but how to learn; how to navigate a world that was not always designed for minds like ours. My mother became our first teacher of metacognition, literacy in my family was never just about reading and writing. It was about knowing yourself well enough to find your own way through.

---

## How do you want to simulate this for students in your classroom?

I want to be the bridge between learning and the real world, the person who stands at the gate and says: you belong here, and the way your mind works is not an obstacle, it is an asset. I want my students to understand that thinking differently is not a limitation. It is a form of intelligence the world does not always know how to recognize yet. What makes them different is exactly what makes them capable of doing things others cannot imagine. My classroom will be a space where neurodivergence, multilingualism, chronic illness, and complex identity are not things to manage around; they are things to build with.

---

## What thoughts are circulating in terms of what counts as literacy?

I have come to understand that literacy is anything that creates, carries, or transmits knowledge. Coding, Storytelling, and Video gaming are all forms of literacy. The same goes for translating for my family, debugging an algorithm, and oral tradition. In other words, if it builds meaning, grows understanding, or helps someone navigate the world, to me it counts as literacy. To me, the question is not whether something is literacy, but whether the institution is willing to see it.
