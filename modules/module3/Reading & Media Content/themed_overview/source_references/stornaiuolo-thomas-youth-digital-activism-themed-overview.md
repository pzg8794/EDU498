# Comprehensive Themed Overview: Stornaiuolo & Thomas (2017)

**Module:** Module 3 / Week 5: Digital & Media Literacy  
**Source:** Stornaiuolo, A., & Thomas, E. E. (2017). *Disrupting educational inequalities through youth digital activism*.  
**Course lens:** Puzzle Plan + EQUITAS  

---

## Source Purpose

This source frames youth digital activism as literacy practice. It shows that young people use digital spaces to participate in civic life, build community, circulate ideas, challenge inequities, and create counter-narratives.

For EDU498, this reading matters because it moves digital literacy beyond technical skill. Digital literacy becomes a way for students to speak, organize, respond, critique, and participate in the world.

---

## Main Ideas

The reading argues that youth are not passive digital consumers. They use digital spaces to name issues, connect with others, create public messages, and challenge dominant narratives. These practices are literacies because they require composing, interpreting, audience awareness, platform knowledge, rhetorical choices, and community participation.

The reading also reminds teachers that digital activism can expose inequalities. Digital spaces can reproduce exclusion, but they can also become tools for students to create visibility and social response.

---

## Puzzle Plan Connection

This source connects strongly to agency and criticality.

- **Access:** Digital tools can give students routes into public conversation.
- **Identity:** Students can create messages rooted in lived experience, culture, language, and community.
- **Agency:** Students become authors, organizers, and public participants.
- **Criticality:** Students question systems, media narratives, and inequitable structures.
- **Transformation:** Literacy becomes a tool for social participation and change.

---

## EQUITAS Connection

EQUITAS asks whether students are being positioned as passive recipients or active knowledge-makers. This reading supports the second position. An EQUITAS-aligned classroom invites students to use digital tools to investigate issues, develop arguments, create counternarratives, and participate ethically in public discourse.

---

## Teaching Implications

Teachers can build projects where students analyze a community issue, examine media representation, create digital advocacy artifacts, produce podcasts or videos, design infographics, or write public-facing multimodal pieces. The key is that students use digital literacy for voice, not only task completion.

---

## Placement Connection

This connects to the Grade 4 Minecraft peer-support example because digital participation became communal rather than isolated. Students used knowledge to support others, which is a smaller classroom version of digital literacy as community action.

---

## Final Themed Takeaway

Stornaiuolo and Thomas help Module 3 frame digital literacy as civic agency. Through the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS, digital activism shows that students can use literacy to become visible, speak back to systems, and participate in collective transformation.
