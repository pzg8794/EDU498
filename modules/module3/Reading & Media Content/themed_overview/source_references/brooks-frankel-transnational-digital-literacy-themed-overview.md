# Comprehensive Themed Overview: Brooks & Frankel (2020)

**Module:** Module 3 / Week 5: Digital & Media Literacy  
**Source:** Brooks, M. D., & Frankel, K. K. (2020). *Pathways for Educators to Challenge Deficit Perspectives: Adolescents' Transnational Digital Literacy Practices in the Classroom*.  
**Course lens:** Puzzle Plan + EQUITAS  

---

## Source Purpose

Brooks and Frankel focus on adolescents' transnational digital literacy practices and how educators can challenge deficit perspectives. The reading asks teachers to recognize the digital, linguistic, cultural, and family-based literacies students already use outside school.

For EDU498, this source matters because it directly supports culturally and historically responsive literacy. It reminds teachers that students' out-of-school digital practices are not distractions from learning. They can be evidence of knowledge, identity, responsibility, and cultural intelligence.

---

## Main Ideas

The reading challenges deficit views by showing that students may already be doing complex literacy work across countries, languages, communities, and platforms. Students may translate for family, maintain relationships across borders, follow news or culture from another place, participate in bilingual communication, or use social media to connect with communities beyond school.

These practices are literacies because they require interpretation, audience awareness, translation, code-switching, cultural knowledge, and digital navigation.

---

## Puzzle Plan Connection

This source connects to the Puzzle Plan through identity and access.

- **Access:** Teachers should build from literacies students already have.
- **Identity:** Transnational and multilingual digital practices are part of who students are.
- **Agency:** Students can use digital tools to move between communities and languages.
- **Criticality:** Teachers must challenge school assumptions that treat home/community literacies as less valuable.
- **Transformation:** Difference becomes a resource for learning rather than a deficit.

---

## EQUITAS Connection

EQUITAS asks which student is being treated as normal. Brooks and Frankel challenge the assumption that the default student is monolingual, locally rooted, English-dominant, and disconnected from transnational networks.

An EQUITAS-aligned classroom recognizes that students' family communication, multilingual digital activity, and cross-cultural navigation are meaningful literacies.

---

## Teaching Implications

Teachers should invite students to bring community knowledge, multilingual practices, family literacies, and digital experiences into classroom work. Students might create bilingual digital artifacts, analyze media from multiple cultural contexts, compare how different communities discuss the same topic, or use digital storytelling to represent transnational identity.

---

## Final Themed Takeaway

Brooks and Frankel help Module 3 challenge deficit perspectives by showing that students' digital practices across language, culture, and community are real literacy. Through the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS, this reading supports classrooms that treat transnational identity as brilliance rather than inconvenience.
