# Melo-Pfeifer & Gertz (2022): Critical Intercultural News Literacy

## Readable Themed Overview

Melo-Pfeifer and Gertz focus on how students read media about minoritized communities. Their reading argues that media literacy cannot stop at fact-checking. Students also need to ask how people are represented, whose voices are missing, what context is left out, and how language shapes public understanding.

This source matters for Module 3 because digital media does not only inform students; it also teaches them how to see people. A headline, image, missing perspective, repeated phrase, or simplified story can shape how a group is understood. Critical intercultural news literacy gives students tools to notice those patterns instead of accepting them as neutral.

Through the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS lens, this reading is protective literacy. Students need tools to question stereotypes, incomplete stories, and narrow public narratives. This is especially important in a course where literacy is connected to identity, access, dignity, and transformation. Students should learn to read not only what a text says, but what it assumes.

The reading also gives teachers a practical approach: problematic media can become a learning resource when handled carefully. Students can compare sources, analyze framing, identify missing voices, and create more accurate or humanizing counternarratives.

## Teaching Takeaway

Teach students to ask deeper media questions: Who is represented? Who is missing? What story is being repeated? What context is absent? What would a more complete and responsible version look like?
