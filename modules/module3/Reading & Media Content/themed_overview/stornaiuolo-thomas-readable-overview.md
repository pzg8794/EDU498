# Stornaiuolo & Thomas (2017): Youth Digital Participation

## Readable Themed Overview

This reading shows that digital literacy is more than technical skill. Young people use digital spaces to speak, connect, organize ideas, respond to events, and create public meaning. Those actions are literacy practices because they require audience awareness, language choices, design decisions, platform knowledge, and thoughtful communication.

The source matters for Module 3 because it moves students from passive technology users to active participants. Students are not only consuming media. They can use digital tools to explain what they notice, represent their communities, ask questions, and create messages that matter.

Through the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS lens, this reading is about agency. A classroom should not assume that school writing is the only serious literacy. Students may already be practicing complex digital communication outside school. Teaching should help them make those practices more critical, ethical, purposeful, and connected to learning.

## Teaching Takeaway

Digital literacy should give students voice. A strong project might ask students to study an issue, compare media messages, create a digital campaign, produce a podcast, design an infographic, or write a multimodal response.
