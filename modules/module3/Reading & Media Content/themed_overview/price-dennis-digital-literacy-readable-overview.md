# Price-Dennis, Holmes, & Smith (2015): Digital Literacy in an Inclusive Classroom

## Readable Themed Overview

This reading explains that digital tools matter only when they help students participate more fully. Technology is not automatically inclusive. It becomes useful when it gives students more ways to communicate, create, collaborate, and show understanding.

The reading pushes against one-size-fits-all literacy. Students can show thinking through images, speech, video, drawing, design, collaboration, and digital composition. That matters because a single written response does not always show the full range of what a student understands.

For our Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS lens, the reading is about access by design. Teachers should ask whether the digital task opens more pathways into learning, or whether it simply puts the same narrow assignment on a screen.

This connects to our placement examples because circuits, Minecraft, and airplane/fan-circuit work all gave students multiple ways to participate. Students learned by building, explaining, helping, testing, and trying again.

## Teaching Takeaway

Use digital tools to widen participation. Let students show understanding through writing, speaking, drawing, recording, building, collaboration, and reflection.
