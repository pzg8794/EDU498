# Dr. Nichole Pinkard: Digital Literacy

## Readable Themed Overview

Nichole Pinkard helps us understand digital literacy as an ecosystem, not a single classroom skill. Students do not become digitally literate just because they use devices. They need access to tools, mentors, communities, authentic audiences, creative projects, and feedback.

This matters for Module 3 because it shifts digital literacy away from task completion. The question is not only whether students can use a platform. The deeper question is whether students have chances to create, design, publish, revise, collaborate, and connect their digital work to real purposes.

Through the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS lens, Pinkard's work is about pathways. Some students have many digital opportunities outside school, while others depend on school to open those doors. Equity is not only device access. It also includes time, guidance, belonging, encouragement, feedback, and meaningful reasons to create.

This connects to the placement examples because students learned most deeply when they were makers. In Minecraft, circuits, and airplane/fan-circuit design, students were not just receiving information. They were building, testing, helping, and seeing their work matter.

## Teaching Takeaway

Digital literacy should give students real opportunities to create. Strong teaching includes tools, mentorship, audience, feedback, and projects that connect school learning to students' lives and communities.
