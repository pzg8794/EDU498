# Common Sense Education: Digital Citizenship

## Readable Themed Overview

The Common Sense digital citizenship materials frame digital literacy as everyday participation. Students are not only using devices; they are reading screens, messages, images, comments, search results, group chats, profiles, and online communities. Digital citizenship teaches students how to participate in those spaces with care, judgment, responsibility, and awareness.

This matters for Module 3 because digital citizenship is often reduced to rules: do not share passwords, do not be mean online, do not believe everything you see. Those rules matter, but they are not enough. Students also need to understand privacy, audience, identity, digital footprints, information quality, media balance, and community responsibility.

Through the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS lens, digital citizenship becomes access work. Teachers should not assume every student enters the classroom with the same digital experience, adult support, language background, confidence, or sense of safety online. Digital citizenship should give students language for making choices, asking questions, setting boundaries, and supporting others.

The source also connects to the Grade 4 Minecraft placement example. When students who finished early helped classmates, digital literacy became community responsibility instead of individual task completion. That is digital citizenship in practice.

## Teaching Takeaway

Teach digital citizenship as literacy, not just behavior. Students should practice reading digital spaces, evaluating messages, protecting privacy, communicating with care, and using technology to support community.
