# Class Activity 2: Deconstructing Headlines

## Headline Analyzed

**Source:** Media Matters  
**Headline:** *The real victims in the “Libs of TikTok” discourse are the teachers and LGBTQ people harassed because of the account*

---

## 1. Break down the headline structure

**Biased language, emotional appeals, and framing techniques:**

- **“Victims”** → clickbait-style framing that elicits mixed responses and immediately asks the reader to think about harm.
- **“Discourse”** → makes the issue sound like a larger public debate or conversation, not just one article or one account.
- **“Because of the account”** → creates a culture-war/debate framing and suggests a cause-and-effect relationship.
- **“Harassed”** → a strong, emotionally charged word that suggests harm, danger, and targeting.
- **“The real victims”** → implies that people are focusing on the wrong issue and that the article is correcting the public conversation.

---

## 2. Skim the article: Does it confirm the initial thoughts?

Yes. The article confirms our first impression because it focuses on:

- Real people being harmed.
- People being named, targeted, or harassed.
- Right-wing media and how the issue is being framed.
- Social justice framing.
- Harm, harassment, accountability, and anti-LGBTQ narratives.

---

## 3. Media Bias Chart placement: Were we surprised?

- Media Matters is rated **Strong Left**.
- We were **not surprised**.
- The headline uses clear **social justice framing**.
- Words like **“real victims”** and **“harassed”** show clear bias/framing.
- Bias does **not** automatically mean fake.

---

## 4. Key takeaways to share out

- **“Real victims”** creates sympathy.
- **“Harassed”** suggests harm and targeting.
- The headline has clear bias/framing.
- Bias does **not** mean fake, but it does mean we should check evidence, framing, and reliability.
