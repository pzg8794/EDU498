## Questions

1. How can AI platforms be able to understand the “why” behind students' mistakes, especially in non-text-based subjects like math?
2. What specific skills and outlooks can we develop now in order to be more useful in our changing role alongside developments in AI? Based on Sămărescu et al.
3. How can schools and governments make sure that AI improves educational opportunities for all students rather than exacerbating existing inequalities between wealthy and developing regions?

---

## Proposed Solutions

### Critical Thinking

Teaching students how to use AI in a way that they question every step of it. Students should use AI critically and structurally. If we teach students how to use AI critically, they can use it for brainstorming, coming up with a plan based on AI drafts, and correcting their work based on what they came up with and what AI suggested.

This helps students identify quality resources, complete tasks efficiently, and question information that is provided by AI. We also need to question what skills we are focusing on.

### Structured Learning

AI should be used within a clear structure so that students know when and how to use it while being responsible for their understanding.

### Skill-Targeted Learning

Teachers can use AI to help identify the skills that their students need most.

## Additional Question

Based on most college academic policies on the use of AI, should K-12 teachers encourage students to use AI for homework assignments and classwork?

---

## Proposed Solutions

We suspect most students use AI for school, and we are sure some use AI for homework and classwork uncritically. Piter believes the solution is to shift focus from whether AI should be used to how it should be used, since students will likely be put in situations where they feel they have to use AI as a resource, especially for time management.

Clear and documented issues exist with students relying on AI for education, but ultimately K-12 teachers are caught between the need to continue teaching traditional fundamentals and the need to teach students how to critically use AI tools. Because of this, we identify criticality as the key skill students need to develop.

However, we still question how educators are supposed to teach students how to wisely and critically use tools that are so new to us as educators and constantly evolving.
