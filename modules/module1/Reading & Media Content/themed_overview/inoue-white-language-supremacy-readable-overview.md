# Asao B. Inoue (2019): White Language Supremacy

## Readable Themed Overview

Inoue's chair address asks what language does in schools and academic spaces. The central issue is not only grammar or correctness. The deeper issue is whose language gets treated as legitimate, intelligent, professional, or worthy. When one language tradition is treated as the standard, students who speak, write, or think through other language practices can be misread as less capable.

For EDU498, this source matters because literacy assessment is never neutral. A student's language carries culture, history, identity, family, geography, and power. If schools reward only one version of academic English, then literacy becomes a gatekeeping tool instead of a pathway into meaning.

Through the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS lens, this source connects directly to CLD students, multilingual students, Dominican/Latino identity, and students whose communication does not match the default school expectation. The question becomes: are we teaching students to communicate across contexts, or are we punishing them for not sounding like the institution already expects?

## Teaching Takeaway

Teach language as power, choice, audience, and identity. Students need access to academic language, but they should not be told that their home language or cultural language practices are broken.
