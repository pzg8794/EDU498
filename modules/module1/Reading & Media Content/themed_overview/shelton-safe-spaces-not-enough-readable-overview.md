# Jama Shelton (2019): Safe Spaces Are Not Enough

## Readable Themed Overview

Shelton's video pushes the same Module 1 question from another angle: a safe space is not enough if the structure underneath the space does not change. Calling a place safe does not automatically make it safe for students whose identities, bodies, languages, families, or histories have been misunderstood or excluded.

This source matters because it asks teachers to move beyond slogans. A classroom can say it welcomes everyone and still leave students unsupported. Real safety requires relationships, policies, accountability, trust, and daily practices that make belonging visible.

Through the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS lens, Shelton helps us see that access is not only about entering a room. It is about whether students can participate without having to shrink themselves, hide parts of themselves, or carry the burden of explaining their humanity. For students who are CLD, neurodivergent, chronically ill, trauma-impacted, or navigating multiple identities, the difference between a claimed safe space and a lived safe space matters.

## Teaching Takeaway

Do not just name a classroom as safe. Build conditions that make safety real: clear norms, trust, accountability, student voice, relationship, and action when harm or exclusion appears.
