# Class 8 Activity 1 — Extending the Discussion Board

**Name:** Piter  
**Class Focus:** Towards a Pursuit of Skills  
**Discussion Focus:** Multilingualism, cultural pluralism, disciplinary literacy, translanguaging, culturally sustaining pedagogy, and computer science participation

## What themes were you seeing?

- Students do not enter classrooms with just one literacy identity.
- Teachers should expand participation instead of limiting students to one language or one "correct" way to show learning.
- In computer science, the goal is not perfect English; the goal is whether students can code, debug, navigate tools, and demonstrate understanding.
- When students are forbidden from using home languages, they may stop participating altogether.

## Connections?

- **Below-grade texts:** Spycher et al. show that multilingual or immigrant-background students are sometimes given below-grade texts because teachers assume they cannot handle complex disciplinary language. This can limit their access to disciplinary concepts instead of supporting growth.
- **Five-day spiral process:** Spycher et al.'s five-day spiral process shows that disciplinary language is built through repeated, scaffolded work with the same text. Students return to words, meaning, discussion, culture, and disciplinary thinking over time.
- **Culturally sustaining pedagogy:** Spycher et al. (2020) show that students should not have to leave home language, culture, community knowledge, or identity behind. Those resources should help students access disciplinary language, skills, and ways of thinking.
- **Translanguaging:** Jin and Liu show that students' full language repertoires, including home languages, dialects, images, emojis, and digital tools, can support meaning-making rather than distract from learning.

## Intersections?

- **Skills + identity:** Students need skill instruction, but those skills should connect to who they are and how they communicate.
- **Disciplinary literacy + access:** Students should learn the language of the discipline without being denied access because of language background.
- **Multilingualism + participation:** Spanish, Swahili, Mandarin, English, or another language can help students engage with the content goal.
- **Assessment + equity:** Teachers may need translation tools, peer support, or other resources to assess multilingual work fairly.
- **Computer science connection:** Coding, debugging, screenshots, design choices, peer talk, and reflection can all count as literacy practices.

## Notes from Final Breakout-Room Discussion

This response was revised using the final breakout-room conversation from the Class 8 transcript. The group specifically discussed:

- A student being questioned for using Spanish in a computer science context.
- A school in East Africa where Swahili was prohibited, which limited student engagement.
- The importance of focusing on the lesson goal rather than policing language use.
- The concern that multilingual student work can be difficult to assess fairly when the teacher is monolingual.
- The idea that below-grade texts can insult students and limit access to complex disciplinary concepts.
- The five-day spiral process as a way to stay with one text and deepen understanding over time.
- Culturally sustaining pedagogy as a way to let students bring home languages and identities into the classroom.
