# Matt de la Peña and Dr. Tracy Flores Video — Source Notes

**Source:** `Default_NWP Radio Presents_ The Write Time with .txt`  
**Video:** *NWP Radio Presents: The Write Time with Matt de la Peña and Dr. Tracy Flores*  
**Used for:** EDU498 Class 8 Asynchronous Activity 1 — The Interplay of Love, Adversity, and Disciplinary Skills

## Key ideas used in the reflection

### Love exists alongside adversity

Matt de la Peña explains that his first version of *Love* felt too simple because it did not acknowledge adversity. He revised the book because love felt more truthful when it included struggle, difficulty, and the ways people grow closer through hard moments.

**Use in Piter's reflection:** This supports the claim that love is not the absence of loss or struggle; love can be struggle transformed into care, persistence, and future action.

### Adversity can deepen connection

De la Peña describes how difficult moments can bring people closer together, including family members, friends, and children navigating overwhelming situations.

**Use in Piter's reflection:** This supports the idea that adversity, when held inside care, can become a learning architecture rather than only a wound.

### Writing and storytelling can heal and connect

The conversation emphasizes writing as a way to process life, document struggle, and build community. Flores frames stories as healing, medicine, and reminders of family strength. De la Peña describes writing letters during crisis as a way of connecting with students, writers, teachers, and families.

**Use in Piter's reflection:** This supports connecting disciplinary communication, reflection, debugging, and data storytelling to love and resilience.

### Stories can be rewritten

The speakers discuss how systems often write people’s stories for them, sometimes in harmful ways. De la Peña describes characters ripping up files and refusing to let institutional narratives define them.

**Use in Piter's reflection:** This connects strongly to algorithmic fairness: systems classify people, but ethical design asks whether those classifications harm, erase, or misrepresent them.

### Representation matters

Flores describes the emotional importance of seeing books and characters that reflect bicultural and multilingual children. De la Peña adds that these books matter not only for children who see themselves represented, but also for children and adults in power who need to encounter those stories.

**Use in Piter's reflection:** This supports the claim that computer/data science systems must be designed to see people fully, not reduce them to narrow categories or biased outputs.

## Key takeaway

The video frames love as active, truthful, and connected to adversity. Love is not fake comfort. It is the willingness to sit with difficulty, document it, respond to it, and build a more humane future from it.
