## Smoke (2017): Identity & Post-Secondary, a First Nations Experience

### Source Location

- Module 4 / Class 7 assigned video
- Smoke, A. (2017). "Identity & post-secondary: a First Nations experience" [TEDx video].

### Working Summary

This video fits the identity pursuit because it centers post-secondary education as an identity negotiation, especially for Indigenous / First Nations students. The key classroom relevance is that students do not enter educational spaces as only academic performers. They enter with histories, communities, cultural responsibilities, family expectations, and experiences of being represented or misrepresented by institutions.

For Module 4, the video helps frame identity as survival and continuity. Post-secondary education can create opportunity, but it can also pressure students to translate themselves into institutional norms. A culturally responsive teacher should not ask students to leave community identity at the door in order to succeed.

### Puzzle Plan / EQUITAS Connection

- **Puzzle Plan:** identity, healing, access, agency.
- **EQUITAS:** asks whether school structures require assimilation before recognition.
- **Teaching connection:** design literacy work where students can connect academic learning to community, place, language, history, and future responsibility without forced disclosure.

## National Writing Project: The Write Time with Matt de la Pena and Tracey Flores

### Source Location

- Module 4 / Class 8 assigned media
- National Writing Project, *The Write Time* episode with author Matt de la Pena and literacy educator Dr. Tracey Flores.

### Working Summary

This media source supports the skill and identity pursuits by connecting writing to voice, story, mentorship, and classroom relationship. Matt de la Pena's work often emphasizes young people's inner lives, family histories, masculinity, class, culture, and emotional complexity. Tracey Flores's literacy work often centers young people's writing lives, culturally sustaining pedagogy, and the importance of students seeing writing as a meaningful act rather than just a school requirement.

For Module 4, the teaching takeaway is that writing skills grow when students have real reasons to write, meaningful audiences, and opportunities to connect writing to identity and community. Skill-building should not remove emotion or story from literacy; it should help students gain tools to communicate what matters.

### Puzzle Plan / EQUITAS Connection

- **Puzzle Plan:** skill, identity, joy, agency.
- **EQUITAS:** asks whether students have multiple meaningful entry points into writing and whether classroom writing treats their lives as worthy material.
- **Teaching connection:** use mentor texts, writer's notebooks, oral rehearsal, drawing, peer conversation, and multimodal drafting to help students build writing skill without flattening voice.

## Lyiscott (2018): Why English Class Is Silencing Students of Color

### Source Location

- Module 4 / Class 9 assigned video
- Lyiscott, J. (2018). "Why English class is silencing students of color" [TEDx video].

### Working Summary

Lyiscott's video pairs strongly with Baker-Bell and Metz. It challenges English classrooms that silence students of color by treating dominant language norms as the only legitimate way to sound intelligent. The core relevance is not only grammar; it is voice, identity, power, and whose language practices are allowed to carry knowledge.

For Module 4, this video supports the argument that English class can either widen students' voices or discipline them into silence. Students need access to academic language, but they also need critical language awareness and permission to use the full range of their linguistic resources.

### Puzzle Plan / EQUITAS Connection

- **Puzzle Plan:** identity, criticality, healing, transformation.
- **EQUITAS:** asks whether classroom language norms create dignity or forced masking.
- **Teaching connection:** invite language analysis, code-meshing, spoken word, bilingual/multilingual expression, rhetorical choice, and discussion of language power.

## The Equity Experience: Critical Literacy and Identity with K-12 Students with Dr. Kazembe

### Source Location

- Module 4 / Class 10 assigned listening/media source
- *The Equity Experience*: Critical Literacy and Identity with K-12 Students with Dr. Kazembe.

### Working Summary

This media source fits the criticality pursuit. It likely centers how critical literacy can help K-12 students examine identity, justice, representation, and power. The key classroom takeaway is that critical literacy is not only a high school or college practice. Students can learn to ask critical questions when teachers provide language, structure, safety, and age-appropriate materials.

For Piter's work, this connects directly to the question of how young students can analyze systems without being overwhelmed. Critical literacy can begin with concrete questions: Who is in the story? Who is missing? Who gets to help? Who gets blamed? What could be more fair? What would we redesign?

### Puzzle Plan / EQUITAS Connection

- **Puzzle Plan:** criticality, identity, agency, healing.
- **EQUITAS:** asks whether students are given tools to name unfairness rather than internalize it.
- **Teaching connection:** use stories, classroom situations, images, design problems, and media examples to help students ask justice-oriented questions.

## On Being: Jason Reynolds, Fortifying Imagination

### Source Location

- Module 4 / Class 10 assigned media
- *On Being* interview with Jason Reynolds, "Fortifying Imagination."

### Working Summary

This media source supports criticality and joy through imagination. Jason Reynolds's work often treats young people's inner lives, language, community, grief, humor, and imagination as intellectually serious. The phrase "fortifying imagination" is useful for Module 4 because imagination is not escapism. It can be a survival tool, a literacy tool, and a justice tool.

For teaching, this connects to the idea that students need opportunities to imagine alternatives. Critical literacy should not only name harm; it should also help students imagine more humane possibilities.

### Puzzle Plan / EQUITAS Connection

- **Puzzle Plan:** joy, criticality, healing, transformation.
- **EQUITAS:** asks whether classrooms make space for imagination as evidence of intellect.
- **Teaching connection:** use creative writing, speculative questions, storytelling, design challenges, and counter-narratives to help students imagine otherwise.

---

## Final Media Takeaway

Across the Module 4 media sources, the shared theme is that literacy is voice, identity, power, imagination, and belonging. Students should not have to become less themselves to succeed. They need classrooms where language, story, community, disciplinary skill, and critical questioning help them become more visible and more powerful.
