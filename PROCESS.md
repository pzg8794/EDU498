# EDU498 Process Guide

## Course Working Theme

EDU498 is organized through Piter's Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS framework. The course is not only about completing literacy assignments. It is about building a connected body of work around culturally and historically responsive literacy, access, identity, healing, joy, criticality, and educational transformation.

Every module should connect back to this question:

> How can literacy teaching become more culturally responsive, historically grounded, accessible, joyful, critical, and healing for students who are usually misread by school systems?

## Core Lens

When reading, writing, reflecting, or creating assignments, keep the following identities and conditions visible:

- neurodivergent students,
- culturally and linguistically diverse students,
- Dominican / Latino students,
- multilingual and transnational students,
- chronically ill students,
- students navigating depression or mental health needs,
- trauma-impacted students,
- disabled students or students with access needs,
- students living with multiple overlapping identities or conditions.

The goal is not to mention every identity in every paragraph. The goal is to avoid generic literacy work and consistently ask how literacy practices include, exclude, label, heal, or empower students.

## Puzzle Plan Workflow

Use the Puzzle Plan as the thinking structure for course work.

### 1. Identify the puzzle piece

Ask what the assignment or reading contributes to the larger project:

- Does it explain access?
- Does it explain identity?
- Does it explain joy?
- Does it explain criticality?
- Does it explain trauma, healing, or belonging?
- Does it explain digital, visual, oral, embodied, or multimodal literacy?

### 2. Connect it to the student experience

Ask how the reading or assignment applies to students who are misread by schools because of body, brain, language, culture, trauma, health, emotional state, or multiple identities.

### 3. Connect it to teaching practice

Ask what a teacher should do differently because of this insight. The answer should usually involve design, access, relationship, multiple modes, criticality, or student agency.

### 4. Connect it to placement evidence when useful

Use placement examples when they strengthen the assignment:

- Kindergarten circuits and fan lessons: embodied, collaborative, vocabulary-rich science literacy.
- Grade 4 Minecraft: digital literacy, peer teaching, persistence, and community responsibility.
- Grade 5 airplane / fan-circuit design: role-based collaboration, design literacy, problem-solving, and multimodal participation.

### 5. Connect it to the final course arc

Each module should help build a reusable framework for future teaching and research. Do not treat assignments as isolated submissions when they can become part of a larger portfolio.

## EQUITAS Workflow

Use EQUITAS as the equity check for all work.

Before finalizing any artifact, ask:

1. **Equity:** Who benefits from the design, and who may be excluded?
2. **Questioning assumptions:** What default learner is being assumed?
3. **Understanding identity:** How are language, culture, body, history, trauma, or neurodivergence shaping learning?
4. **Inclusive access:** What supports make participation possible?
5. **Transformative literacy:** How does the work expand what counts as literacy?
6. **Agency:** How do students gain voice, choice, authorship, or power?
7. **Sustainability:** How can this idea become part of future teaching practice, not just one assignment?

## Drafting Process

### Keep labels until the end

When drafting assignments, keep headings and assignment labels visible until Piter says to combine or polish. This helps verify that every required section is covered.

### Avoid petty edits

Do not over-edit wording that already works. Focus on:

- missing assignment requirements,
- real grammar issues,
- unclear logic,
- weak transitions,
- missing course connections,
- missing Puzzle Plan / EQUITAS connections.

### Use surgical edits when requested

When Piter asks for surgical changes, provide the smallest meaningful change possible. Do not rewrite the whole section unless asked.

### Preserve rhythm and voice

Piter's strongest writing often combines personal narrative, systems critique, teaching practice, and academic framing. Preserve that voice. Do not flatten it into generic academic prose.

### Combine only after structure is complete

First draft with labels. Then tighten. Then combine into the final required format.

7. Teaching implication.

## Structure Hygiene Check

Before finalizing module-level reorganization or reading-pipeline updates, run:

```bash
python3 scripts/check_module_consistency.py
```

This validates:

- singular `Reading & Media Content` directory usage,
- absence of deprecated plural directory names,
- presence of core reading pipeline folders/files,
- source inventory expected-count alignment with themed overview counts when declared.

## Final Quality Check

Before calling any assignment complete, verify that it answers:

- What does this say about literacy?
- What does this say about access?
- What does this say about identity?
- What does this say about students who are misread by school systems?
- What does this change about teaching practice?
- How does it connect to Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS?
