# Activity 3 Source Trail

This note documents how the revised EDU 498 Class 2 Activity 3 draft was built.

## Assignment Prompt

The prompt came from `module2/Summer A 2026 Class 2 Defining Literacies.docx`.

Activity 3 asks for two asynchronous responses:

1. **Building on the Basics** - What is missing from the New Basics, and how can the New Basics be expanded using this module's readings? Include 1-2 Culturally and Historically Responsive foundations.
2. **New Learners and New Teachers** - What is missing from the New Teachers definitions, and how can New Teachers be defined in ways that better meet New Learners' needs? Include 1-2 Culturally and Historically Responsive teaching approaches.

## Current Draft Claim

The revised response uses this central claim:

> Literacy should be understood as critical participation, especially in digital and multimodal spaces.

The response connects literacy to print, speech, listening, code, interface navigation, design, AI use, peer feedback, debugging, and classroom participation.

## Course Reading Threads

The draft also connects to the Class 2 readings and media listed in `module2/Readings & Media Content.pdf`:

- Bishop's mirrors, windows, and sliding glass doors supported the point that students need texts and literacy practices that reflect them, expand their understanding, and let them enter new worlds.
- Mackey's question of who reads what, in which formats, and why supported literacy as situated, purpose-driven, and format-diverse.
- Muhammad's Culturally and Historically Responsive framework supported the use of criticality and intellect.
- Souto-Manning and Yoon supported the idea that children read and write their worlds across home, community, language, body, race, and power.

## Draft Evolution

The first draft emphasized "literacy as access to participation" and named Identity, Criticality, Skills, and Joy.

The revised draft sharpened that into:

- Part 1 foundation: **Criticality**
- Part 2 teaching approach: **Intellect**
- Main concept: **critical participation**
- Placement anchor: Pine Brook IGNITE digital/CS literacy
- Pedagogical claim: students are capable knowledge-makers when teachers design rigorous access into the structure of learning

## Privacy Guardrails

- No student names are used.
- No transcript excerpts are reproduced.
- The draft uses broad placement patterns and teaching reflections rather than identifying classroom stories.
- The source trail points to sibling repositories for local continuity, but it does not copy private materials into this repo.
