# Designing a Minecraft World That Carries Identity and Community Knowledge

**Course / Project:** EDU498  
**Student focus:** Ethan, Computer Science  
**Status:** Collaborative draft  

## Purpose

This document is a live collaborative draft version of the mini-unit. It can be copied into HackMD for shared editing, then synced/exported back into GitHub.

## Core idea

Students use Minecraft Education to design a meaningful digital place that communicates identity, community knowledge, belonging, memory, or future possibility. The unit treats coding, debugging, design, screenshots, oral explanation, and written reflection as connected literacy practices.

## Student profile

Ethan comes from a Seneca family and is proud of his Haudenosaunee heritage. He is also 25% white and sometimes feels unsure about how to represent both parts of himself. He enjoys computer science, coding, robotics, game design, problem-solving, building simple games, learning programming languages, and exploring emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence. He is especially interested in how digital tools can support community engagement, storytelling, and the preservation of cultural knowledge.

## Five pursuits brainstorm

### Identity

Students design a Minecraft place that can represent identity, community, memory, belonging, or future possibility. For Ethan, this creates space to connect coding and game design with Seneca / Haudenosaunee heritage, mixed identity, outdoor connection, community storytelling, and cultural knowledge without forcing private disclosure.

### Joy

Joy comes through choice, building, testing, debugging, peer support, visible progress, and sharing a world students created. Minecraft makes joy part of the learning structure because students can see their ideas become playable and meaningful.

### Skills

Students practice sequencing, decomposition, debugging, spatial reasoning, MakeCode / Minecraft Education tool use, planning before building, screenshot use, partner communication, and oral or written explanation.

### Intellect

Students build understanding that code and design communicate meaning. They think about how paths, signs, structures, colors, repeated patterns, and interactions guide an audience through a digital world and tell a story about identity or community.

### Criticality

Students begin asking whose stories are represented in digital worlds, whose knowledge is missing, how technology can preserve or erase community stories, and what respectful use means when designing with cultural symbols, community knowledge, or shared histories.

## Disciplinary writing component

Students complete a short Developer Design Note:

- My design goal was...
- The build/code steps I used were...
- One bug or revision I worked through was...
- One feature I want my audience to notice is...
- This world communicates identity, community, belonging, or future possibility by...

## Three-lesson arc

1. **Plan a Meaningful Place** — choose a place concept, sketch meaningful features, decompose the build into steps, and draft a design goal.
2. **Build, Code, and Debug** — build, test, revise, and document one coded or repeated feature that helps the world communicate meaning.
3. **Share, Explain, and Reflect** — present the world, submit a Developer Design Note, explain design choices, receive feedback, and revise one feature for clarity, welcome, or accessibility.

## Notes for collaborators

When editing this document, protect student agency and privacy. Do not require students to reveal personal, cultural, family, or community knowledge. The assignment should assess intentional design and explanation, not disclosure.
