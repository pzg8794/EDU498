# Comprehensive Themed Overview: Serpagli & Mensah (2021)

**Module:** Module 3 / Week 5: Digital & Media Literacy  
**Source:** Serpagli, L. P., & Mensah, F. M. (2021). *Keeping up with the digital natives: Using social media in an all-girls science classroom*.  
**Course lens:** Puzzle Plan + EQUITAS  

---

## Source Purpose

Serpagli and Mensah explore the use of social media in an all-girls science classroom. The reading connects everyday digital practices to science learning and asks how familiar platforms can support student engagement, communication, and science identity.

For EDU498, this source matters because it connects digital literacy, STEM learning, gender, and classroom access. It shows that digital tools can become bridges between students' everyday literacies and academic content.

---

## Main Ideas

The reading challenges the idea that digital tools are separate from serious academic learning. Social media-style practices can help students document thinking, share observations, communicate with peers, represent scientific ideas visually, and connect science to lived experience.

At the same time, the reading requires caution. Teachers should not assume that all students are equally confident, safe, or supported online just because they are young. Digital familiarity is not the same as critical digital literacy.

---

## Puzzle Plan Connection

This source connects to access, joy, and identity.

- **Access:** Social media-style tools can create familiar entry points into science learning.
- **Identity:** Students can see themselves as science learners through digital expression.
- **Agency:** Students can create, post, explain, respond, and document learning.
- **Joy:** Digital creation can make science more social, visual, and connected.
- **Criticality:** Students still need guidance around audience, privacy, credibility, and responsibility.

---

## EQUITAS Connection

EQUITAS asks whether the classroom assumes a default STEM learner: confident, fast, verbally assertive, and already comfortable with academic science language. This reading suggests that digital tools can open additional pathways, especially when students are invited to use visuals, informal explanation, collaboration, and familiar platforms.

An EQUITAS-aligned classroom uses digital tools with boundaries, accessibility, and explicit instruction instead of assuming every student already knows how to use them critically.

---

## Teaching Implications

Teachers can use social media-inspired activities such as science posts, digital lab reflections, short videos, visual explanations, class discussion boards, or collaborative documentation. These activities should include clear expectations for privacy, respectful participation, evidence, and scientific accuracy.

---

## Final Themed Takeaway

Serpagli and Mensah help Module 3 connect digital literacy to STEM participation and identity. Through the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS, the reading supports classrooms where technology creates access, expression, and joy while still being guided by critical digital citizenship.
