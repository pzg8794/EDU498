# Serpagli & Mensah (2021): Social Media in a Science Classroom

## Readable Themed Overview

Serpagli and Mensah connect digital literacy to science learning. Their reading shows that social media-style practices can help students document ideas, share observations, explain concepts visually, and participate in science through familiar forms of communication.

The reading matters because STEM classrooms often reward a narrow type of participation: confidence, speed, technical vocabulary, and polished answers. Digital tools can widen that space. Students may be able to show science understanding through images, short explanations, posts, comments, videos, or collaborative documentation before they can express it in traditional academic language.

Through the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS lens, this source is about access, identity, and joy. Social digital practices can help students see themselves as science learners, especially when the classroom lets them connect everyday communication with academic inquiry. At the same time, the reading reminds us that teachers should not assume all students are equally safe, confident, or critical online. Digital familiarity is not the same as digital literacy.

This connects to the placement work because the circuit and airplane activities also made science visible, physical, and collaborative. Students learned by testing, building, watching, explaining, and sharing success.

## Teaching Takeaway

Use digital formats to make science more participatory. Students can create visual explanations, short digital reflections, collaborative posts, or media-based science notes, but teachers must still teach privacy, audience, evidence, and respectful participation.
