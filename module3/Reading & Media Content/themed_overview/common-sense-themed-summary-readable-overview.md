# Common Sense Digital Citizenship Themed Summary

## Readable Themed Overview

This source takes the Common Sense Digital Citizenship curriculum and reads it through the course theme: literacy as identity, access, community care, and critical participation. It focuses especially on how digital spaces shape identity, how repeated messages can build stereotypes, and how students can learn to respond when online spaces become unfair or harmful.

The strongest idea is that digital citizenship is not just private behavior. It is community literacy. Students need to understand how posts, comments, images, profiles, algorithms, group chats, and repeated claims influence the way people are seen. They also need language for responding with care instead of silence, embarrassment, or reaction without reflection.

Through the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS lens, this source helps explain why digital citizenship must include bias, confirmation bias, representation, and upstander communication. Students who are often misread in school can also be misread online. A responsive classroom gives students tools to question what they see, recognize unfair patterns, and participate in ways that protect dignity and belonging.

This source also fits the placement pattern. In Grade 4 Minecraft, students used digital knowledge to support classmates. That is the positive version of digital citizenship: technology used for access, peer support, and shared success.

## Teaching Takeaway

Digital citizenship should teach students how to recognize identity pressure, repeated assumptions, and unfair digital patterns. It should also give them practice responding as thoughtful community members, not just rule followers.
