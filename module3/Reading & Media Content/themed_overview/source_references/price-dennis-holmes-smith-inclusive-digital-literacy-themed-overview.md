# Comprehensive Themed Overview: Price-Dennis, Holmes, & Smith (2015)

**Module:** Module 3 / Week 5: Digital & Media Literacy  
**Source:** Price-Dennis, D., Holmes, K. A., & Smith, E. (2015). *Exploring digital literacy practices in an inclusive classroom*.  
**Course lens:** Puzzle Plan + EQUITAS  

---

## Source Purpose

This source focuses on digital literacy in an inclusive classroom. It treats technology as part of access, participation, and meaning-making rather than as a reward or separate enrichment activity.

For EDU498, this source is central because it helps connect digital literacy to inclusion. Students can use digital tools to compose, explain, collaborate, represent understanding, and participate in ways that traditional print-only literacy may not capture.

---

## Main Ideas

The reading shows that digital literacy practices can open multiple pathways for students. Instead of assuming that every student must demonstrate learning through the same mode, inclusive digital literacy creates opportunities for students to use images, sound, oral language, visual design, assistive tools, collaboration, and digital composition.

The reading also pushes teachers to consider classroom design. Digital tools are not automatically inclusive. They become inclusive when teachers use them intentionally to reduce barriers, increase participation, and support meaningful communication.

---

## Puzzle Plan Connection

This source connects strongly to the Puzzle Plan as an access layer.

- **Access:** Digital tools can reduce barriers when students have multiple ways to participate.
- **Identity:** Students can represent themselves and their thinking through different modes.
- **Agency:** Students gain more control over how they communicate understanding.
- **Joy:** Digital creation can support confidence, experimentation, and shared success.
- **Transformation:** Students are no longer limited to one narrow version of academic performance.

---

## EQUITAS Connection

EQUITAS asks teachers to examine whether the classroom design assumes one default learner. This reading challenges that assumption by showing that inclusive digital literacy should begin with flexible participation and multiple modes of expression.

An EQUITAS-aligned classroom does not ask whether students can fit the tool or task exactly as designed. It asks whether the design gives students real access to meaning-making.

---

## Teaching Implications

Teachers should design digital literacy lessons that allow students to show understanding through writing, speaking, drawing, recording, visual design, peer collaboration, and digital creation. The key is to use technology to deepen access and expression, not simply to make a traditional worksheet digital.

---

## Placement Connection

This source connects to the Kindergarten circuit work, Grade 4 Minecraft, and Grade 5 airplane/fan-circuit design because those placement moments expanded literacy through tools, collaboration, explanation, and hands-on participation.

---

## Final Themed Takeaway

Price-Dennis, Holmes, and Smith help Module 3 frame digital literacy as inclusive design. Through the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS, the reading supports classrooms where students can show understanding through multiple pathways rather than being judged by one narrow literacy performance.
