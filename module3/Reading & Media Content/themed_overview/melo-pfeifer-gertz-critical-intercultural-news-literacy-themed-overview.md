# Comprehensive Themed Overview: Melo-Pfeifer & Gertz (2022)

**Module:** Module 3 / Week 5: Digital & Media Literacy  
**Source:** Melo-Pfeifer, S., & Gertz, H. (2022). *Transforming Disinformation on Minorities Into a Pedagogical Resource: Towards a Critical Intercultural News Literacy*.  
**Course lens:** Puzzle Plan + EQUITAS  

---

## Source Purpose

Melo-Pfeifer and Gertz focus on disinformation about minorities and how teachers can transform problematic media into a resource for critical intercultural news literacy. The reading moves media literacy beyond simple fact-checking and asks students to examine representation, power, culture, language, and missing context.

For EDU498, this source matters because it helps students understand how media narratives shape the way groups are seen. Literacy includes reading headlines, images, framing, omissions, and assumptions.

---

## Main Ideas

The reading argues that students need tools to analyze news and digital media critically. They should ask who is represented, who is missing, what assumptions are being repeated, what historical context is absent, and how language shapes public understanding.

The reading also turns disinformation into a teaching opportunity. Instead of ignoring harmful or incomplete media, teachers can use it carefully to help students identify bias, compare sources, build intercultural understanding, and create more responsible counternarratives.

---

## Puzzle Plan Connection

This source connects strongly to criticality and healing.

- **Access:** Students need tools to read media systems, not only individual articles.
- **Identity:** Students examine how groups are represented and misrepresented.
- **Healing:** Critical literacy helps students avoid internalizing narrow or damaging narratives.
- **Agency:** Students can create more humanizing responses and counternarratives.
- **Criticality:** Students question power, framing, omission, and audience.
- **Transformation:** Media literacy becomes a way to interrupt deficit narratives.

---

## EQUITAS Connection

EQUITAS asks whose story is treated as normal and whose story is treated as suspect, incomplete, or invisible. This source supports an EQUITAS-aligned classroom by teaching students to examine how media constructs credibility, belonging, and difference.

Teachers should help students analyze media without reducing students to examples of marginalization. The goal is to build critical tools, dignity, and intercultural understanding.

---

## Teaching Implications

Teachers can ask students to compare media sources, analyze headlines, study image choices, identify missing perspectives, trace claims, and create revised or counternarrative media artifacts. Students should learn to ask not only whether information is true, but also how it frames people and communities.

---

## Final Themed Takeaway

Melo-Pfeifer and Gertz help Module 3 frame media literacy as protective and transformative literacy. Through the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS, students learn to read media critically, recognize harmful patterns, and produce more accurate, humanizing narratives.
