# Common Sense Digital Citizenship: Themed Summary

## Source
- Common Sense Education: Digital Citizenship Curriculum
- URL: https://www.commonsense.org/education/digital-citizenship

## My Theme
I read this set through one theme: literacy as critical participation in digital spaces.
I focus on bias, stereotypes, and online harm.

## My Core Takeaway
I see digital citizenship as identity work, power awareness, and community responsibility.
What we post, share, like, and amplify shapes social meaning.
That pattern influences how people read gender, race, ethnicity, and belonging.

## Key Theme Threads I Pulled

### 1. Identity and Stereotype Formation
- "Beyond Gender Stereotypes" names direct stereotype pressure.
- "Who Are You Online?" and identity lessons show how repetition creates labels.
- Audience reaction, visibility, and platform signals shape how identity gets read.

### 2. Hate Speech and Community Harm
- "Responding to Online Hate Speech"
- "Countering Hate Speech Online"
- "The Consequences of Online Hate Speech"

These lessons push me toward action.
I see harm as a community issue that needs community response.

### 3. Information Habits and Confirmation Bias
- "Challenging Confirmation Bias"
- "Filter Bubble Trouble"
- "Clicks for Cash"
- "Hoaxes and Fakes"

I connect these lessons to stereotype reinforcement.
When a feed keeps confirming one story, prejudice gains credibility.

### 4. Upstander Literacy
- Upstander and communication lessons frame intervention as a literacy practice.
- Students build language for naming harm and responding with care.

I value this because response habits build safer learning spaces.

## Questions I Use While Reading
1. Who gets centered in this message?
2. Who gets reduced to a stereotype?
3. What evidence supports the claim?
4. What platform feature increases spread?
5. What upstander response fits this moment?

## Reflection-Ready Moment
My strongest insight is that online stereotype harm grows through everyday participation routines.
Identity labeling, feed design, and peer language work together.
I respond by centering media literacy, identity dignity, and upstander communication in classroom practice.

## Discussion Prompts
1. How does online identity performance shape stereotype pressure in youth culture?
2. Which classroom move helps students respond to hate speech with clarity and care?
3. How does confirmation bias strengthen harmful assumptions across difference?
4. How can digital citizenship lessons support culturally and historically responsive literacy practice?
