## Assignment Framing

Possible social justice issue:

> Neurodivergent and chronically ill students can be excluded from advanced STEM learning when course communication assumes one default learner: someone who can decode vague policies, tolerate hidden process changes, disclose medical context repeatedly, respond well to public questioning, and adapt to a single preferred communication mode.

Literacy-as-power angle:

- Syllabus literacy: who can find and interpret requirements when they are buried or verbally changed?
- Accommodation literacy: who knows how to invoke accommodations without over-disclosing?
- Scientific communication literacy: whose ways of organizing, coding, visualizing, and explaining data are treated as legitimate?
- Institutional literacy: who knows when to use DSO, Ombuds, advisor mediation, or reporting pathways?
- Critical literacy: how do classroom communication norms reproduce ableism even when the instructor may intend to help?

Puzzle Plan / EQUITAS connection:

- Access: unclear requirements, hidden process changes, and single-mode expectations become barriers.
- Identity: neurodivergence, chronic illness, CLD identity, accent, age, and prior technical expertise shape how communication is interpreted.
- Healing: the assignment can critique communication patterns without turning the professor into the whole story.
- Agency: the evidence shows a student seeking DSO, advisor, peer, and faculty support to regain control.
- Criticality: the issue is not only one interaction; it is how institutional literacy and classroom literacy distribute power.
- Transformation: the final paper can propose inclusive communication protocols that help all students, not only students with formal accommodations.

## Inclusive Communication Strategies to Propose

- Put every requirement in one accessible written location; do not rely on verbal-only changes.
- Use plain-language summaries for assignments, deadlines, exceptions, and grading rules.
- Give students multiple communication channels, but define which channel controls deadlines and requirements.
- Allow multimodal evidence of thinking when handwriting, typing, drawing, code, or speech each carries different access burdens.
- Treat DSO accommodations as the floor, not the whole imagination of accessibility.
- Keep disability-related corrections private and adult-to-adult; avoid public questions that force disclosure or imply incompetence.
- Use structured check-ins: "What did you understand the requirement to be?" and "What part of the process is unclear?"
- Build peer-team norms for direct clarification instead of triangulated communication through the instructor.
- Train STEM faculty on neurodivergent communication differences, chronic illness variability, and the difference between rigor and rigidity.

## Language to Use Carefully

Safer phrasing:

- "The transcript records a student reporting that..."
- "A DSO-related conversation frames the issue as..."
- "The evidence suggests a communication-access problem..."
- "The concern is not only whether an accommodation existed, but whether the classroom communication design made the accommodation usable."

Avoid in polished submission:

- Personal insults.
- Claims about intent unless a source directly establishes intent.
- Naming the professor unless the assignment requires it and Piter approves.
- Long copied transcript excerpts. Paraphrase and cite the source link/line instead.
