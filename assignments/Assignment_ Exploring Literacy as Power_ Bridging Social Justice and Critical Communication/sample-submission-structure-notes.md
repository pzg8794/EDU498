# Sample Submission Structure Notes

## Purpose

This document records takeaways from the sample submission folder shared for the assignment. The purpose is not to copy the sample's content, topic, or wording. The purpose is to understand the expected assignment shape, level of directness, and section organization so the final paper can meet the rubric while preserving Piter's own voice and evidence base.

## Key Structural Takeaway

The relevant sample uses a direct, rubric-aligned structure:

1. Topic / social justice issue
2. Research & Documentation
3. Critical Analysis
4. Social Media / present-day movement or initiative
5. Target audience
6. Expected literacy levels
7. Accessibility strategies
8. Potential communication barriers
9. Recommendation
10. Critical Reflection

This confirms that the assignment does not require a hidden or overly complex academic structure. A clear paper structure is acceptable, and direct labels can work as long as the writing itself is thoughtful.

## What This Means for Our Paper

Our paper should remain more polished and integrated than the sample, but it should not become so complex that the rubric disappears. The sample validates the need to make the required components visible.

For our paper, use polished versions of the rubric categories:

1. **Opening / Statement of Truth**
2. **Research Findings: Communication Access as a Social Justice Issue**
3. **Critical Analysis: Literacy as Communication Power**
4. **Connecting the Issue to the Present: UDL, Teaching Placement, and Student Vulnerability**
5. **Recommendations: Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS Communication Design**
6. **Critical Reflection: From Pain to Protection**

## Research Findings Adjustment

The sample's `Research & Documentation` section is source-focused and fairly direct. This supports our current plan to include:

- **Identify the Topic**
- **Why This Issue Matters**
- **Relevant Sources and Evidence Scope**

Do **not** add a separate conclusive subsection in Research Findings. The Critical Analysis section should do the interpretation.

## Critical Analysis Adjustment

The sample's Critical Analysis focuses on how jargon, academic language, tone, and field-specific terminology create barriers for audiences. Our Critical Analysis should do the same, but with our evidence:

- syllabus language;
- verbal-only requirements;
- DSO / Flex Plan / Ombuds language;
- scientific jargon such as reference genome, RNA-seq, NovaSeq, flat file, differential expression;
- grading phrases such as `10 out of 12` and `first ten submissions`;
- team communication expectations through Slack, Google Docs, task trackers, and peer evaluations;
- how these literacies affect neurodivergent, chronically ill, CLD, trauma-impacted, depressed/anxious, and K--12 students differently.

## Present-Day Connection Adjustment

The sample uses a social media movement and then analyzes:

- target audience;
- expected literacy levels;
- strategies used to make information accessible;
- potential communication barriers.

For our paper, the present-day connection should use:

- **Universal Design for Learning / CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0** as the initiative;
- **teaching-placement transcripts** as the living classroom bridge;
- screenshots from the UDL initiative/page.

This section must explicitly analyze:

- target audience: teachers, professors, teacher educators, students, support staff, K--12 learners, neurodivergent and chronically ill students;
- expected literacy levels: not high/low only, but scientific, institutional, digital, disability, language, emotional, and self-advocacy literacy;
- accessibility strategies: multiple means of representation, engagement, action/expression, plain language, visuals, multimodal supports, stable information;
- barriers: jargon, digital access, professional abstraction, lack of translation into concrete classroom communication practices.

## Recommendation Adjustment

The sample recommendations are simple and practical: multimedia, simpler language, translation, print alternatives.

Our recommendations should also stay practical, not overly theoretical. Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS should organize the recommendations, but they must produce concrete actions:

- stable written requirements;
- plain-language assignment summaries;
- verbal updates repeated in writing;
- explicit communication-channel hierarchy;
- multimodal demonstration of knowledge;
- private access-related clarification;
- group communication norms;
- visual assignment maps;
- student-facing DSO/Ombuds/advising guide;
- community dialogue and team conflict protocols.

## Reflection Adjustment

The sample reflection synthesizes the issue, research, campaign, barriers, recommendations, and personal learning. Our reflection must do the same, but at a deeper level.

Our reflection should synthesize:

- the BIO/STEM evidence;
- the adult-with-resources case;
- the danger for children and less-resourced students;
- EDU498 readings;
- UDL as the present-day initiative;
- teaching-placement observations;
- Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS recommendations;
- why literacy mediates social justice.

## Final Takeaway

The sample confirms that the assignment rewards clarity and explicit coverage of the rubric. Our final paper can be more emotionally powerful and analytically rigorous than the sample, but it should still make the required pieces easy for the instructor to find.

The goal is:

> polished enough to feel like Piter's journal, clear enough to satisfy the rubric, and structured enough that the social justice argument cannot be missed.
