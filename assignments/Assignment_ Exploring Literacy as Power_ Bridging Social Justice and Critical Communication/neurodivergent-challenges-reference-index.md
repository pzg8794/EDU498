### T1 · Modern Literacy Education, Multiliteracies, and Inclusive Curricula
**Link:** [transcripts/2026-05-21 Lecture: Modern Literacy Education, Multiliteracies, and Inclusive Curricula](https://github.com/pzg8794/EDU498/blob/main/transcripts/2026-05-21%20Lecture_%20Modern%20Literacy%20Education%2C%20Multiliteracies%2C%20and%20Inclusive%20Curricula-transcript.txt)

**What it documents:**
- A student discloses in real time that she has insomnia lasting 3–4 days when medication fails, that her body has "adapted" to it, and that overmedication is her only tool to recover sleep — while simultaneously completing graduate coursework (timestamp ~11:30–12:40). This is a documented instance of a neurodivergent/chronically ill student navigating academic demands without any institutional accommodation being made visible in the classroom structure.
- Students collectively reject the idea that there is one valid form of reading: "To shoot someone down in the way they choose to read is defeating the purpose of encouraging literacy" (Speaker 2, ~01:35). This directly maps to the deficit model critique and its impact on neurodivergent learners for whom audiobooks, graphic novels, or oral text are primary access points.
- A student notes that "district and state requirements kind of negatively force educators to fit in a box and encourage specific types of reading over others" (~22:10) — naming the structural mechanism that produces one-size-fits-all pedagogy and its downstream harm.
- The professor introduces the "fourth grade slump" (~47:18) as the point where students who lack academic language prototypes fall behind — a moment with particular consequence for neurodivergent students whose executive function, processing speed, or language acquisition follows non-linear timelines.
- A student with multiple processing styles describes needing to pair audiobook + visual text simultaneously (~04:23) — lived evidence of multimodal access need.
- The hip-hop literacy video (~50:00–53:00) demonstrates joyful, multimodal, culturally grounded literacy that implicitly challenges the print-dominant, compliance-first model that excludes neurodivergent learners.

**Puzzle Plan Dimensions:** Access · Identity · Healing · Agency · Joy · Transformation
**EQUITAS Questions Answered:**
- Whose language, body, pace, attention, and emotional state is being assumed by the default classroom design?
- What forms of literacy are being recognized, and which are being ignored?

**Scholarly Framing:**
> *The May 21 EDU498 transcript captures what Cope and Kalantzis describe as the gap between "old basics" and "new basics" — and reveals that even in progressive classrooms, students with chronic illness and neurodivergence are still navigating that gap largely alone, disclosing medical need informally (to a professor after class) rather than through a system designed to hold that information.*

### P1 · ED452C Readings Summary — Neurodivergence, CLD Learners & Multiple Identities
**Link:** [Drive / readings-summary-discussions-1-3.md](https://github.com/pzg8794/EDU498/blob/main/readings-summary-discussions-1-3.md) *(Google Drive source, mirrored context)*

**What it documents — by discussion:**

**Discussion 1 — Reframing Disability as a Design Problem:**
> *"A student who is neurodivergent…is not the problem to be fixed. The problem is an environment that was designed for a narrow range of learners."*
- Executive-function demands, sensory environment, pacing, and direction structure as access barriers misread as capability problems.
- The inclusive move is to redesign the route into the goal, not lower the goal.

**Discussion 2 — The "Default User" Problem in Educational Technology:**
> *"For neurodivergent students, open-ended digital environments can create executive-function challenges that look like disengagement or resistance but are actually problems of task structure and cognitive load."*
- Intersecting identities (multilingual + neurodivergent) face compounding barriers that neither label fully captures.
- Equity requires asking not just "Does every student have a device?" but "Does the design give every student a real path into the thinking?"

**Discussion 3 — Person-Environment Fit and the Launch Routine:**
> *"For neurodivergent students, the warm-up and transition into a task are often the most difficult moments — not because the content is too hard, but because the executive-function demand of shifting attention and decoding a new set of directions is highest at the start."*
- Wehmeyer and Kurth (2021): student difficulty at the launch is diagnostic data about the environment, not evidence of low ability.
- "Stuck looks the same on the surface regardless of whether the barrier is a login error, language load, executive-function demand, or limited prior experience."

**Puzzle Plan Dimensions:** Access · Healing · Criticality · Transformation
**EQUITAS Questions Answered:** All seven questions directly addressed across the three discussions.

**Scholarly Framing:**
> *These summaries synthesize Wehmeyer and Kurth's person-environment fit framework with the EQUITAS architecture, arguing that neurodivergent students' struggles are most accurately read as diagnostic data about classroom design failures rather than individual deficits.*

### P2 · Alex's Student Profile — Comprehensive Analysis Through Trauma-Informed and Neurodiversity-Affirming Lenses
**Link:** [Drive / Alex's Student Profile](https://github.com/pzg8794/EDU498/blob/main/assignments/Alex-s-Student-Profile.md) *(Google Drive source)*

**What it documents:**
- A 17-year-old student with ASD whose ADHD presentation was missed across multiple evaluations — documented evidence of **diagnostic overshadowing** as a systemic failure.
- The failure to identify co-occurring conditions "contributes to cascading effects on self-esteem, self-advocacy, and academic engagement" — the downstream cost of one-size-fits-all assessment.
- Behavioral reframing table:
  - "Lack of motivation" → Chronic fatigue and learned helplessness from repeated invalidation
  - "Immaturity" → Self-protective coping mechanisms
  - "Dependence on adults" → Seeking safety in the only available support structure
  - "Resistance to help-seeking" → Prior experiences of being dismissed
- "When educational teams approach complex learners like Alex with curiosity rather than judgment, and with recognition of neurodiversity rather than deficit-thinking, they create opportunities for genuine growth."

**Puzzle Plan Dimensions:** Healing · Identity · Agency · Transformation
**EQUITAS Questions Answered:**
- Which students are being labeled because the classroom design is too narrow?
- How can instruction interrupt deficit narratives instead of reproducing them?

**Scholarly Framing:**
> *Alex's case operationalizes what Sapon-Shevin calls the dangerous lesson taught by exclusion: when systems label neurodivergent students as unmotivated or immature, those labels follow students through their educational careers and into the world, reproducing the very deficits they claim to identify.*
