## Assignment Use

Use these threads to support a social justice issue around communication access:

- Hidden or verbal-only requirements can exclude students who rely on written records, transcripts, and explicit process documentation.
- Accommodation literacy is not only about knowing the DSO process. It is also about whether classroom communication makes the accommodation usable.
- Scientific communication literacy includes code, documentation, visual formats, reports, and team coordination. When only one mode is treated as legitimate, students with different access needs are put at risk.
- A "one-size-fits-all" classroom may unintentionally treat memory, handwriting, speed, and tolerance for ambiguity as signs of seriousness or competence.

## Evidence Handling

This evidence can carry anger and still be precise. The point is not to make the experience sound polite. The point is to make the pattern legible as a communication-access problem.

Useful phrasing:

- "The email record shows repeated requests for clarification and health-related process support."
- "The handwritten peer-evaluation thread is evidence of a mismatch between verbal instruction and accessible written requirements."
- "The Week 7 and Week 4 threads show how grading-language ambiguity became an access issue."

For formal claims, mark the difference between documented fact, student/peer perception, and interpretation. That distinction protects the force of the argument; it does not require softening the feeling.
