# Class 4 – CHRE Commitment Reflection
## EDU498 | Piter Garcia Bautista

---

Culturally and Historically Responsive Education (CHRE) is not a framework I came to from a textbook — it is the framework I needed when I was the student. As a Dominican, neurodivergent, multilingual learner navigating U.S. schools, I experienced firsthand what happens when education treats narrow standards of performance, behavior, and communication as universal. CHRE means recognizing that students carry histories, languages, identities, and ways of knowing that are not deficits to overcome but literacies to build from. It means that the curriculum, the classroom routines, and the teacher’s gaze must expand — not so students can fit the system, but so the system can finally see them. For me, CHRE also means interrupting the trope of the “inconsistent student,” a trope that erased my neurodivergence behind labels like lazy or careless, compounded by a cultural masculinity that told me struggle was weakness. CHRE is the commitment to read students more honestly than I was read.

I am currently in a teaching placement, and my commitment is specific: I will design at least one unit this term where students have multiple entry points to demonstrate understanding — not only written or verbal, but visual, computational, and embodied. I will treat inconsistency in student performance as data that asks a question, not evidence that closes a case. I will build in explicit moments where students can name what they need to access the learning, modeling that naming your needs is not weakness but literacy. Drawing on Sealey-Ruiz’s Archaeology of Self, I will also create space for students to excavate what school has taught them about who belongs — so they can begin to interrupt those beliefs the way I am still interrupting mine. Culturally and Historically Responsive Literacy, in my classroom, will not be a unit or a lesson. It will be the architecture of how the room works and what it tells every student who walks in: you do not have to become less yourself to be seen as capable here.
