## EDU498 | Piter Garcia Bautista

## Reflect on My Learning

I have learned that Culturally and Historically Responsive Education means recognizing that students' languages, histories, identities, families, bodies, and lived experiences shape how they learn and how they participate. CHRE pushed me to think about literacy beyond reading and writing; literacy can include storytelling, navigating expectations, interpreting systems, translating identity, technology usage, visuals creation, collaboration, problem-solving, and making meaning from lived experience. In my teaching placement, I have seen this clearly when students used circuits, Minecraft, and other activities to show understanding through doing, helping, explaining, building, and trying again — not just through traditional written answers.

---

## My Commitment

I am committed to implementing and expanding the principles of CHRE. I know what it feels like to be misunderstood by systems that value consistency, compliance, and visible performance more than students that battle invisible enemies to participate. As a Latino (Dominican), neurodivergent, and chronically ill student that navigates multiple medical conditions and identities, I understand that students are not lacking because they learn differently. Often, schools lack the structures, imagination, and relationships needed to read students fully. My commitment is to help build learning spaces where students do not have to not be themselves in order to be seen as capable; a gate to transition their "differences" from inconvenience to assets, toward brilliance. This commitment also comes from my teaching placement, where I have seen students become more confident when lessons gave them roles, materials, peer support, and more than one way to participate.

---

## My Plan to Implement CHRL

CHRE means teaching in a way that recognizes students as whole people shaped by culture, history, language, identity, family, body, and lived experience. Furthermore, it means expanding literacy beyond reading and writing to include storytelling, decoding expectations, navigating systems, translating identity, using technology, creating visuals, building with materials, collaborating with classmates, and making meaning from experience. I saw how in Kindergarten circuit lessons, students were not only learning about wires, switches, batteries, and fans; they were learning through touch, observation, shared vocabulary, and a collective countdown that made the science experience visible and memorable. I am committed to CHRE principles because I know what it feels like to be misunderstood by systems that value consistency, compliance, and visible performance over the invisible enemies students fight to participate. As a Latino (Dominican), neurodivergent, and chronically ill student that navigates multiple medical conditions and identities, I understand that students are not lacking because they learn differently; schools often lack the structures and relationships needed. I observed this in my teaching placement: students showed brilliance when the classroom gave them access. In Grade 4 Minecraft, students who finished were asked to help classmates who were not done, turning digital literacy into peer teaching and community responsibility.

In my teaching, I will implement CHRL by designing lessons where students can bring their cultures, languages, questions, identities, and ways of thinking into the classroom. I will provide multiple ways to show understanding through discussion, storytelling, writing, drawing, technology, collaboration, building, peer teaching, and reflection. I will also treat inconsistency as information, not failure, by asking what support students need instead of labeling. In practice, this means designing lessons like the Grade 5 airplane/fan-circuit activity, where students had different roles, built parts of a shared object, solved problems with materials, and participated through teamwork instead of one narrow form of literacy.
