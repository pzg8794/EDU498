# Class 4 – Cultural Pedagogies (prep overview)

## Big throughline
Cultural pedagogies are not about "adding culture" to school. They redesign school so students’ languages, histories, identities, and ways of knowing are sustained and treated as legitimate knowledge.

## Muhammad (2020) – Chapter 2 (38–61)
- **Core argument:** literacy is tied to identity, intellect/knowledge, skills, and criticality (with joy as the design requirement in this course).
- **What Muhammad is pushing:** culture is tied to historical memory and agency; students shouldn’t leave their identities at the door to be counted as literate.
- **Classroom implication:** plan for identity + knowledge + critique + joy, not just performance.

## Paris & Alim (2014) – Culturally sustaining pedagogy
- **Core argument:** move from culturally relevant to culturally sustaining pedagogy (CSP): sustain students’ linguistic, literate, and cultural practices (plural, evolving) rather than treating them as hooks.
- **The "loving critique":** earlier asset pedagogies can still slide into assimilation; CSP centers heritage and contemporary youth culture while still inviting critical reflection.
- **Key warning:** schooling often measures students of color through the White middle-class gaze; CSP asks what would be sustained if the school was designed around students’ actual community practices.

## Strekalova-Hughes & Wang (2019) – Refugee-background family storytelling
- **Core argument:** family storytelling is a culturally sustaining literacy practice; it builds identity, language maintenance, memory, connection, and structure.
- **Why it matters:** refugee-background students are often reduced to trauma/need narratives; this study centers children’s voices and agency instead.
- **Classroom implication:** treat oral, embodied, repeated storytelling as literacy; add cultural context before asking students to interpret/share.

## Ladson-Billings (2021) – 30+ years of CRP/CRRSP/CSP
- **Core argument:** culturally relevant pedagogy has three commitments (learning, cultural competence, critical consciousness). If those are missing, the practice is watered down.
- **Pandemic "reset":** "back to normal" isn’t acceptable if normal already failed many students.
- **Key push:** keep frameworks as frameworks (not scripts); use integrated, problem-based, creative, student-centered work across disciplines (including STEM/design/coding).

## Amanda Gorman (2021) – "The Hill We Climb"
- **Core argument:** speaks from crisis to repair; hope is action and responsibility, not denial.
- **Classroom implication:** poetry as civic literacy; close reading + performance + discussion of who is included/excluded in "we".

## Yolanda Sealey-Ruiz (2022) – Truth, Love & Racial Literacy
- **Core argument:** racial literacy requires truth + love and an "Archaeology of Self" (teachers examine their racial socialization, assumptions, and filters).
- **Why it matters:** cultural pedagogy fails if teachers can’t see what students bring; love does not mean avoiding truth.

## Bridge to our theme
- Class 3 theme: **joy requires institutional recognition and accessibility**.
- Class 4 extension: students can’t experience belonging or joy if their languages, stories, and literacies are only used as hooks or "background." 
- **Belonging before building:** the teacher must design accessible pathways *and* do the self-work (racial literacy) required to recognize students’ practices as literacy.
