## Commitment Statement

My joyful literacy pursuit for the mini-unit is belonging before building. Before any student works independently or in a group, the whole class will build shared understanding together through a contributor + team model: one student per table as the contributor, supported by their group, with vocabulary and concepts named only once students have the materials in their hands. Joy is not added on top of the lesson; it is the structure of the lesson itself. Janet Jackson’s *State of the World* asks who is being left out and why that is acceptable. My mini-unit brings that same question to literacy instruction, because joy that is not structurally accessible is not available to all students. Joy in that literary work is not just celebration; it is a demand. It is what joy looks like when connected to justice, and that is the frame this mini-unit commits to.

## Journal Entry

For my joyful literacy pursuit, I chose to code an interactive Colab notebook built around healthcare AI fairness. This was joyful not because the coding was easy, but because I was designing a learning experience — not just writing code. Every decision required me to think about how students would read the page, move through activities, interpret visuals, respond to fairness scenarios, and make meaning from a tool that affects their real lives.

This connects directly to my mini-unit commitment of belonging before building. The notebook gives learners a structured pathway into difficult ideas before asking them to produce independently. Coding here was a literacy practice because I was using language, structure, visuals, interaction, and feedback to make complex ideas accessible. The joy came from building something that invites students into the learning rather than leaving them outside of it — which is the same demand Janet Jackson’s *State of the World* makes of the systems that shape people’s lives.

Joy that is not structurally accessible is not available to all students. This notebook is my attempt to design for that.
