# Bishop (1990): Mirrors, Windows, and Sliding Glass Doors

## Readable Themed Overview

Bishop gives Module 2 one of its strongest literacy images: texts can be mirrors, windows, and sliding glass doors. A mirror helps students see themselves. A window helps students see into lives and worlds beyond their own. A sliding glass door invites students to enter, imagine, and participate.

Through the Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS lens, this source is about representation as access. Students who never see their languages, families, cultures, bodies, histories, or identities in curriculum may learn that school was not built with them in mind. Students also need windows into other experiences so literacy becomes empathy, imagination, and community understanding.

This source matters because representation is not decoration. It shapes whether students feel visible, valued, and capable of making meaning inside the classroom.

## Teaching Takeaway

Choose texts that work as mirrors, windows, and doors. Students need to see themselves and also encounter others with dignity, complexity, and care.
