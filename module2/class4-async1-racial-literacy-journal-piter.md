# Class 4: Asynchronous Activity 1 Journal
## Unearthing What We've Learned: A Reflective Excavation of Racial Belief
**Name:** Piter
**Class:** EDU498 · Class 4 · Culturally-Historically Responsive Literacies
**Date:** May 28, 2026

---

## Describe the moment
One moment that still stays with me happened during my first semester in the United States. I was in a class discussion where I offered an interpretation of a reading. Before engaging my idea, the response centered on my accent and language delivery. The room shifted from discussing the argument to evaluating whether I sounded "academic enough" to belong in the conversation. I remember feeling that my intelligence had to be translated before it could be heard.

---

## Excavate the moment
The belief underneath that moment was that legitimacy has a single sound: White, monolingual, and institutionally familiar. The assumption centered status, race, and language hierarchy. It decided who gets read as naturally intelligent and who must prove it first. That belief comes from schooling histories that center dominant language as neutral and position bilingual or immigrant speakers as people who need correction.

---

## Reflect on the impact of the moment
The immediate impact was contraction. I spoke less, edited myself more, and shifted energy from learning to self-monitoring. Over time, that pattern shaped belonging: I learned to split parts of myself depending on the room. Looking back now, the emotion is still present, but it has changed. What felt like personal inadequacy now reads as institutional pattern recognition. The moment taught me that exclusion can happen through ordinary interactions and explicit hostility.

---

## Interrupt the moment
My interruption commitment is to design classrooms where students can show full identity and still be recognized as capable. In practice, I name multiple language practices and communication modes as legitimate. I assess ideas before fluency performance. I build participation structures that protect voice diversity. I also practice Sealey-Ruiz's archaeology of self continuously. I examine how I interpret behavior, speech, and "readiness" so my teaching decisions advance equity.

---

## Pattern statement
Recurring pattern: institutions reward identity flattening before granting academic legitimacy.
Chosen interruption target: teacher noticing and assessment language.
