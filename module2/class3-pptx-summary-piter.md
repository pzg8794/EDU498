## Central Argument of the Lecture

Joy is not a mood, a reward, or a break from learning — it is a legitimate and necessary **educational pursuit**. This lecture builds the case that joy must be *designed into* curriculum and instruction, not hoped for. The teacher's role is not to produce joy directly but to create the structural conditions that make joy possible. If those conditions are not built in, joy will not happen for all students — only for those who already feel seen by the existing curriculum.

## Section 4: Digging Deeper — The Five Instructor Questions (Slides 15–18)

The lecture's theoretical core. The instructor presents joy as an **operationalizable educational pursuit** — not vague, not optional, but something teachers must be able to answer concretely:

> 1. How does my curriculum and instruction **enable, amplify, and spread** joy?
> 2. What types of joy could I **bring into** the curriculum and instruction?
> 3. How does my curriculum and instruction **elevate beauty within humanity**?
> 4. How will I write **learning objectives** that will help my students pursue and embrace joy?
> 5. How will I **know** if my students are experiencing the learning practices of joy?

**Why these questions matter:** They are not rhetorical. Question 5 especially — *how will I know?* — demands that joy be visible, assessable, and built into lesson design. A teacher who cannot answer Q5 has not yet designed for joy; they have hoped for it.

## Synthesis: What the Lecture Argues in One Paragraph

Joy in literacy has historical legitimacy going back to the 1700s, but has been systematically suppressed by standardized curriculum. Muhammad's CHRE framework reclaims it as one of five pillars of justice-centered education — not a supplement but a foundation. Joy is not the same as happiness: it is deeper, more revolutionary, and connected to race, identity, and student agency. Teachers must move beyond being skills instructors to becoming designers of conditions — asking not just *what will students learn* but *how will I know joy is happening?* The choice board and mini-unit commitment are not add-ons; they are the instructor's demonstration that joy must be practiced by teachers before it can be designed for students.
