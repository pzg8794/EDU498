## Commitment Statement

My joyful literacy pursuit for the mini-unit is **belonging before building**. Before any student works independently, the whole class will build shared understanding together through a contributor + team model — one student per table as the contributor, supported by their group, with vocabulary and concepts named only once students have the materials in their hands. Joy is not added on top of the lesson. It is the structure of the lesson.

*State of the World* asks who is being left out and why that is acceptable. That is the same question this design asks of literacy instruction. Joy in that song is not a celebration — it is a demand. It is what joy looks like when it is connected to justice, not just comfort. That is the frame this mini-unit commits to.

## The Five Pursuit Questions

**How does my curriculum and instruction enable, amplify, and spread joy?**
By structuring collective ownership before individual production. In my Kindergarten squishy circuits lesson, every student had a hand in the fan circuit before anyone built alone — the fan turning on was a shared payoff, not a solo performance. My mini-unit carries the same logic: shared accomplishment first, independent production second.

**What types of joy could I bring into the curriculum?**
Three types, all documented in placement transcripts:
- *Joy of ownership* — a student who built something and can point to their role in it
- *Joy of breakthrough* — a student who failed repeatedly, adjusted, and finally got it
- *Joy of collective expertise* — a student who taught a peer and watched them succeed

**How does my curriculum and instruction elevate beauty within humanity?**
By choosing the quietest student as the contributor — not the loudest or most confident — distributing intellectual authority rather than reinforcing existing status. Beauty lives in the moment the student who is usually overlooked throws the switch and makes the fan turn on in front of the whole class.

**How will I write learning objectives that help students pursue and embrace joy?**

| Version | Objective |
|---|---|
| Standard | Students will connect components in a simple circuit. |
| Reframed through joy | Students will build a working circuit together, contribute to a shared outcome, and explain one decision they made as a team. |

The reframe adds agency, collaboration, and voice — all entry points for joy the standard alone does not open.

**How will I know if my students are experiencing the learning practices of joy?**
- They arrive at the independent task ready, not anxious — because shared understanding came first
- They correct each other without waiting for the teacher — because they own the knowledge
- They stay with failure because the task matters to them
- One student, somewhere, has that quiet moment — the exhale, the *"I did it"* said to no one in particular

Joy is not loud. It is persistent, self-directed, and self-referential. It will be documented in observation notes, not only in the final product.
